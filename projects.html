Project Thesis Sep 2024 – Apr 2025

Road Friction Estimation During Stationary Steering Maneuvers

Conducted research at the Chair of Automatic Control, FAU-Erlangen, focusing on estimating road surface friction during stationary (low-speed) steering maneuvers on a Steer-by-Wire (SbW) vehicle platform from Bucknell University.

  • Processed and analyzed time-synchronized vehicle sensor data (steering angle, wheel speed, etc.) for localization and maneuver planning in low-speed scenarios.
  • Developed MATLAB/Simulink tire dynamics models for both lateral and longitudinal behavior under stationary and dynamic steering conditions.
  • Created and applied five distinct load distribution models across the tire contact patch to simulate shear stress under real-world constraints.
  • Built and trained machine learning models (FNN, LSTM, GRU) in Python to predict road friction coefficients, enabling improvement over previous physics-based approaches.
